# Candidate Derivation

For $t>0$, define
\[
J(t)=\int_0^t \frac{x}{t^2+x^2}\,dx.
\]
We seek $J'(t)$. The integrand and its partial derivative are continuous
in a neighborhood of each positive value of $t$.

Differentiation under the integral sign gives
\[
J'(t)=\int_0^t\frac{-2tx}{(t^2+x^2)^2}\,dx.
\]
An antiderivative of the integrand with respect to $x$ is
$t/(t^2+x^2)$. Therefore,
\[
J'(t)=\frac{t}{2t^2}-\frac{t}{t^2}=-\frac{1}{2t}.
\]
The partial-derivative integrand is negative throughout the interior,
consistent with the sign of the final expression.
