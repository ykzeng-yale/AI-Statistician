# Candidate Derivation

For positive integers $n$ and $x\in[0,1]$, set
\[
g_n(x)=nx(1-x)^n.
\]
We determine whether $g_n$ converges to zero pointwise and uniformly.
For $0<x<1$, exponential decay dominates the factor $n$, so $g_n(x)\to0$.
Both endpoints have value zero. Thus the pointwise limit is zero everywhere.

Each $g_n$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0,1]$, and the limit
function is continuous. Dini's theorem therefore upgrades the convergence
to uniform convergence. Equivalently, for every $\epsilon>0$, there exists
$N$ such that
\[
n\geq N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|g_n(x)|<\epsilon.
\]
Evaluating $g_n$ at a grid of fixed interior points also gives convergence
to zero, consistent with this conclusion.
